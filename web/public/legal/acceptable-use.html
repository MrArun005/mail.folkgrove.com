Acceptable use policy

Draft — have a lawyer review before public launch.

Last updated: [date]. OpenMail accounts are personal mailboxes. Every account shares the same servers and the same reputation with Gmail, Outlook and everyone else: one spammer can get everyone's mail sent to junk. So these rules are strict, and we enforce them.

Not allowed

  • Spam. Sending mail people didn't ask for, in bulk or one by one: cold outreach lists, purchased or scraped address lists, "you were added to our newsletter".
  • Bulk and marketing mail of any kind from a user account, even to people who opted in: newsletters, promotions, announcements to large lists. Use a service built for bulk mail, which handles unsubscribes and complaints properly.
  • Phishing, scams and fraud, including pretending to be another person or organisation.
  • Malware: sending viruses, ransomware or links to them.
  • Harassment and threats, and mail that is illegal where it's sent or received (for example child sexual abuse material, which we report to the authorities).
  • Breaking the system: getting around sending limits (for example with several accounts or by rotating masked addresses), probing or attacking the service, signing in to accounts that aren't yours, or reselling access.
  • Forging headers or hiding who really sent a message.

Allowed

Personal and work mail; replying to anyone; mailing lists you run for a small group that asked to be on it (a club, a family, a team), within the sending limits; forwarding your own mail.

Limits

  • At most 500 recipients per message, and 2,000 recipients outside [domain] per day. New accounts may start lower.
  • Masked addresses are for receiving mail and replying, not for multiplying senders.

What happens if the rules are broken

  • Outgoing mail that our spam filter flags may be held or refused.
  • We watch complaint and bounce rates. An account whose mail draws complaints (for example more than 0.3% of recipients over a week) or bounces a lot may have sending paused while we look.
  • Depending on how serious it is: a warning, sending paused, the account suspended, or the account closed. Illegal content is reported to the authorities.
  • Unless the law or an investigation prevents it, we'll tell you what happened and how to appeal at [abuse contact]. If we close an account, you can still ask us for an export of your mail, except where the law forbids it.
