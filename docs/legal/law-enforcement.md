# Law enforcement guidelines

**Draft — have a lawyer review before public launch.**

Last updated: [date]. How [Operator name] ("we") handles requests from police, courts and other authorities for information about OpenMail users.

## What we require

- **Valid legal process** issued under the laws of [jurisdiction], served on us at [legal contact address]. For data in another country's jurisdiction, use the mutual legal assistance process or an applicable international agreement.
- **A specific account** (full email address) and a specific, reasonably limited time range and type of data.
- We review every request. We push back on requests that are too broad, unclear or not legally valid, and we may challenge them in court.
- Emergencies: if there is an imminent risk of death or serious harm, write to [emergency contact] explaining the emergency. We may disclose the minimum needed to prevent it, and ask for legal process afterwards.

## What we have

Only what the [privacy policy](privacy-policy.md) lists:

- **Account details:** username, sign-up time, the email address verified at sign-up, masked addresses.
- **Logs:** sign-in times and IP addresses, and sender/recipient addresses and times for messages, kept for [retention period].
- **Stored content:** mail, contacts, calendars and settings currently in the account (and in backups for up to [14] days after deletion). Content requires [a court order / warrant — name the instrument].

We have nothing about deleted accounts beyond the retired address itself (once backups have cycled), no browsing or location history, no AI-derived profiles, and no data from your devices.

## Notifying users

We tell the user before disclosing their data, so they can object, unless a court order or law prohibits it, or telling them would create a risk of death or serious harm. If a gag order ends, we tell the user then.

## Preservation

We will preserve a specific account's current data for 90 days on receipt of a valid preservation request, while legal process is obtained. We don't create data we don't already keep.

## Transparency

We publish [twice a year] the number of requests we received, by country and type, and how many we complied with, in full or in part.
